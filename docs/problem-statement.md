# VivAI — Problem Statement

Right now, when an engineering student needs to prepare for a viva, they open ChatGPT or Google Gemini, paste their project topic, and ask for questions. And it works — for about five minutes. Then the limitations hit: ChatGPT cannot see your actual codebase, cannot listen to your spoken answers in real time, cannot watch your face on camera and tell you that you're speaking too fast or losing eye contact, cannot guide you slide-by-slide through your presentation, cannot track your weak topics across multiple sessions, and cannot switch fluidly between English, Hinglish, or Tanglish in a real-time spoken conversation. Students are left practicing alone in front of a text box — building no muscle memory for the high-pressure oral examination they are about to face. VivAI was built to fill this gap. It runs Live voice engine, so the AI speaks and listens like a real examiner with sub-second latency, then adds six purpose-built capabilities on top: a live mock viva that adapts difficulty and asks project-specific questions in 13 languages including Hinglish and Tanglish; a presentation practice mode where you share your slides and the AI watches your webcam simultaneously, giving real-time feedback on pace, eye contact, and confidence; a communication coach that runs live role-plays for HR interviews, group discussions, and panel Q&A with camera-based sentiment tracking; a code-aware viva that actually reads your uploaded project ZIP or GitHub repo, analyzes the architecture, and asks implementation-level questions like why you chose a particular framework or what happens if a layer is removed; a 90-second pitch drill that times and scores your elevator pitch with structured feedback on framing and delivery; and a weakness heatmap that tracks every question you answer across every session, maps it to topics, and shows exactly which areas are still red so you know what to fix before the real exam. ChatGPT can write your project report. VivAI prepares you to defend it out loud, under pressure, in front of a panel.
